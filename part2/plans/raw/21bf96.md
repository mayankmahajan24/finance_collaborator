## What we are deciding

We are not trying to build an earnings-call sentiment signal in the next two weeks. We are trying to find out, cheaply and honestly, whether one is worth building at all. The output on day ten is a binary: either we have a number that clears a threshold we wrote down before we saw it, and we scope a real build, or we shelve the idea with a written record of why. Everything below is arranged around protecting that decision from the two things that normally corrupt it — running specifications until one works, and mistaking a well-known effect for a new one.

## Hypothesis

The claim under test, stated narrowly enough to be falsifiable: **the tone of management's answers during the Q&A portion of a quarterly earnings call carries information about the firm's return over the following quarter, relative to its industry peers, that is not already contained in the earnings surprise, the market's immediate reaction to the announcement, or the guidance issued alongside it.**

Every clause is load-bearing. "Management's answers in Q&A" because prepared remarks are drafted by IR and legal and are close to a constant per firm; the unscripted portion is where the residual information plausibly lives, and this is where the original literature located it. "Relative to peers" because the question is about relative performance and because absolute tone loads heavily on sector cycles — in 2015-16 every energy call sounded grim, and an unadjusted tone signal would have been a short-energy bet wearing a linguistics costume. "Not already contained in the surprise" because post-earnings announcement drift is one of the most robust anomalies in the literature and tone correlates with the surprise at roughly 0.3; a naive tone sort will recover PEAD and look like a discovery.

The null we expect to fail to reject, honestly, is that the incremental content was real in 2004-2010, has been arbitraged and commoditised since, and now sits at an incremental t-statistic under 2 in recent data. The plan is designed so that outcome is a clean, defensible finding rather than an ambiguous shrug.

## Days 1-3: establish what is already known

Three days, no data work, no code. The deliverable is a four-page memo plus a frozen pre-registration document.

**The literature.** The anchor paper is Price, Doran, Peterson and Bliss (2012, *Journal of Banking & Finance*), "Earnings conference calls and stock returns: The incremental informativeness of textual tone" — they find Q&A tone predicts post-call drift incrementally to SUE over roughly 2004-2007. Read alongside it: Loughran and McDonald (2011, *JF*) for why general-purpose sentiment dictionaries mislabel financial text and for the word lists we will use; Mayew and Venkatachalam (2012, *JF*) on vocal affect in calls, which matters because it suggests part of the signal is in the audio and not the transcript; Larcker and Zakolyukina (2012, *JAR*) on linguistic markers of deception; Bochkay, Hales and Sengupta (2019) on extreme language; Hassan, Hollander, van Lent and Tahoun (2019, *QJE*) for the template of building a firm-quarter measure from transcripts properly; Cohen, Malloy and Nguyen (2020, *JF*, "Lazy Prices") for the closely related finding that *changes* in language predict returns better than levels; Garcia, Hu and Rohrer (2023, *JFE*, "The colour of finance words") on machine-learned replacements for the LM lists; Frankel, Jennings and Lee (2022, *MS*) on dictionary versus ML sentiment extraction; and Ke, Kelly and Xiu on supervised topic-based text scoring as the modern methodological benchmark. On the LLM side, Lopez-Lira and Tang (2023) and Kim, Muhn and Nikolaev (2024) — read primarily for their handling of look-ahead contamination, which is the central methodological hazard of using a modern model on historical text.

**Decay since publication.** This is the most decision-relevant part of the three days and it deserves a full day. McLean and Pontiff (2016) document roughly a 26% in-sample-to-out-of-sample decline and a further 32% post-publication decline for the average published anomaly. Our prior should be that a signal published in 2012, on data ending in 2007, in a domain with cheap machine-readable inputs, has decayed more than the average. The memo should state an explicit prior on the surviving effect size — I would write down "one-third of the published coefficient" — and note that we will estimate the decay curve directly rather than assume it.

**What is already sold.** Half a day of vendor calls and pricing. The relevant list: S&P Global Market Intelligence's Textual Data Analytics, which ships pre-computed sentiment on Capital IQ transcripts including a Q&A/prepared-remarks split; LSEG MarketPsych Analytics; RavenPack (Bigdata.com); Bloomberg's transcript analytics; AlphaSense; Aiera; Alexandria Technology; Brain Company's language metrics; SESAMm; and the lineage of Prattle and Amenity Analytics, both of which were absorbed rather than scaled independently — itself a datapoint about the economics of standalone transcript sentiment. Two questions to each vendor: what is the annual seat cost for a point-in-time history back to 2010, and how many asset-management clients consume it. If a directionally similar score is available for well under the cost of a quant building it, the build case has to rest on a demonstrated edge over the vendor score, not over zero. Note that vendor availability also implies crowding — a signal thousands of subscribers can see is priced faster.

**Pre-registration.** By end of day three, commit to the repository a document specifying: the exact primary regression, the exact tone measure, the universe, the sample period, the peer definition, the control set, the standard-error treatment, the kill thresholds, and the list of robustness checks. Timestamp it with a git commit before touching return data. Nothing in that document may change after day three; anything we later wish we had specified differently gets recorded as an exploratory result, labelled as such, and given no weight in the gate.

## Days 4-10: one specification

**Universe and sample.** Russell 3000 constituents, point-in-time, quarterly earnings calls from January 2015 through the most recent complete quarter. That start date is chosen deliberately: it is fully out of sample relative to the published work and post-dates the period in which most of the commercial products launched, so the test is "does it work now," not "did it ever work." Expect roughly 90,000 firm-quarters. Require at least five peers in the peer set and a minimum price of $5 to avoid a microcap-only result.

**Data.** Transcripts from S&P Capital IQ (via WRDS if available, otherwise a direct licence) with the *transcript publication timestamp*, not just the call date — this is non-negotiable and is where naive versions of this study leak. Earnings actuals and consensus from I/B/E/S for SUE, defined as actual EPS minus the median forecast from the 90 days before the announcement, scaled by the standard deviation of those forecasts (with a price-scaled variant as robustness). Management guidance from I/B/E/S Guidance, coded as the change in the midpoint of full-year EPS guidance versus the prior issuance, scaled by price, with a separate indicator for initiation and withdrawal. Returns and delisting returns from CRSP. Fundamentals from Compustat with a proper reporting lag. GICS sub-industry from the point-in-time Compustat/CRSP linkage; Hoberg-Phillips TNIC-3 as the alternative peer definition.

**Tone measure.** Primary and only: the Loughran-McDonald positive-word count minus negative-word count, scaled by total words, computed over *management speech in the Q&A section only*, with speaker attribution parsed from the transcript's speaker tags and analyst turns dropped. Negation handling per the LM convention (a positive word within three words of a negator is flipped). Then — and this is the specification choice I would defend hardest — the signal is not the level but the *within-firm change*: tone minus the firm's own mean tone over its previous four calls. CEOs have persistent verbal styles, and a level-based sort is partly a cross-sectional sort on personality. Winsorise at 1/99 and convert to cross-sectional deciles within each calendar quarter.

**Primary specification.** A pooled panel regression of the peer-relative cumulative return over trading days [+2, +63] after the transcript timestamp on the tone decile, controlling for the SUE decile, the announcement-window return CAR[-1,+1], the scaled guidance revision and its initiation/withdrawal indicators, log market cap, book-to-market, 12-1 momentum, Amihud illiquidity, analyst dispersion, share turnover, and the firm's prior-quarter tone level. Peer-relative return is the firm's return minus the equal-weighted return of its GICS sub-industry peers over the identical window. Industry-by-quarter fixed effects. Standard errors two-way clustered by firm and calendar quarter, per Petersen (2009); because return windows overlap across firms within a quarter, also report a Fama-MacBeth version with Newey-West lags equal to the horizon in quarters. The number that goes to the gate is the t-statistic on the tone coefficient **in the full specification with SUE, CAR[-1,+1] and guidance included**. The specification without controls will be reported for context and is not decision-relevant.

Note the [+2, +63] window: day 0 and day 1 are excluded so the signal is tradeable at prices available after the transcript is actually published. If the effect exists only in [0,+1], it is not investable at our size and counts as a fail.

**Days 6-7 are a replication, not a new test.** Before pointing the pipeline at recent data, run it on 2004-2010 and try to recover the published result. If our pipeline cannot reproduce a t-statistic near 3 in the original sample, then a null on recent data tells us about our code, not about the market. This is the single highest-value use of two days in the whole plan and it is routinely skipped.

**Day 10 portfolio test.** Convert the signal to a monthly-rebalanced long-short portfolio, top decile against bottom decile within sub-industry, equal-weighted and value-weighted, positions held 60 trading days with overlapping tranches. Report gross and net of 20bp round-trip cost, and report the spread's exposure to the Fama-French five factors plus momentum and short-term reversal.

## The kill criterion, written down in advance

Escalate to a full build only if **all four** of the following hold. Failing any one is a kill.

1. The incremental tone t-statistic in the full primary specification, with two-way clustered errors, is **≥ 2.5**. Given that we are pre-registering exactly one primary test, 2.5 is defensible against the Harvey-Liu-Zhu (2016) argument for a 3.0 hurdle; if we end up reporting three tone variants, the hurdle mechanically rises to 2.9 by Bonferroni, and we will apply it.
2. The net-of-cost long-short spread is **≥ 3% annualised with an information ratio ≥ 0.4** over 2015-present.
3. The annual spread is correctly signed in **at least 7 of 10 years**, and 2020 is not the source of the result — re-run excluding March 2020 through December 2020 and the t-statistic must stay above 2.0.
4. The result is not a microcap artifact: the bottom size quintile contributes **less than 40%** of the total spread, and the sign holds in at least three of four size quartiles.

If it passes, the day 11-14 deliverable is a build scope: infrastructure, latency requirements, capacity estimate, and an explicit comparison against simply licensing the vendor score. If it fails, the deliverable is a two-page negative memo naming the specific residual hypotheses worth a future look — most likely the audio channel, which no vendor prices well and which Mayew and Venkatachalam suggest is where the non-commoditised information sits.

## Validation

Three checks, all pre-specified. A **placebo window**: the same regression on peer-relative returns over [-63, -3] before the call should show no incremental tone coefficient; if it does, we have a stale-characteristic leak. A **pipeline sanity check**: randomly shuffle speaker labels within each transcript and confirm the signal disappears — if a shuffled transcript still predicts, we have a bug or a length artifact. And a **vendor correlation check**: on a one-month trial of the S&P or MarketPsych transcript score, compute the cross-sectional rank correlation with our measure. Above 0.7 and we are rebuilding a commodity; the build case then requires demonstrating incremental content over the *vendor* score, which becomes the new control in the regression.

## Pitfalls I will be watching

The surprise-contamination problem is first and largest: with SUE and CAR[-1,+1] omitted, tone will predict, and it will be PEAD. Second, timestamp leakage — transcripts appear hours to days after the call, and the naive design that assumes same-day availability inflates results in exactly the small illiquid names where the effect appears strongest. Third, sector loading, which the peer-relative construction and industry-by-quarter fixed effects address but which should still be checked by inspecting the sector composition of the extreme deciles. Fourth, dictionary drift: the LM lists were built on 1994-2008 filings, and "headwinds," "transitory" and "reset" have carried different weights in different decades; this is the argument for the within-firm change specification, which differences out slow-moving vocabulary shifts. Fifth, boilerplate — safe-harbour language and legal hedging load negatively and vary with counsel, not with prospects. Sixth, coverage bias — transcript availability is itself correlated with size and analyst following, so the sample is not the universe. Seventh, and the reason the LLM measure is deliberately *not* the primary here: any model trained through 2024 has read what happened after these calls, and there is no clean way to prove a plausible-sounding sentiment score is not partly recall. If we run an LLM variant at all, it goes on a stratified 20% subsample as an exploratory secondary, with a scrambled-date control, and it does not enter the gate. Eighth, overlapping windows inflating t-statistics, handled by the clustering and Newey-West treatment above. Finally, the discipline problem: after day three, no new specification may be promoted to primary. The temptation on day nine, staring at a t of 2.1, to try equal-weighted instead of value-weighted, or [+2,+42] instead of [+2,+63], is exactly the failure mode the pre-registration exists to prevent. A t of 2.1 is a kill.
