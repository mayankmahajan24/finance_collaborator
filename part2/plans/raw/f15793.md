# f15793

## What we are actually asking

"Do CAGR changes drive multiple re-ratings?" is not a testable question as posed, because a rising expected growth path raises value almost by construction, and because the standard way of measuring it is contaminated twice over: analysts revise after prices move as often as before, and the multiple most people reach for (EV/forward EBITDA, forward P/E) has the revised estimate sitting in its denominator, so a revision changes the multiple arithmetically even if no one trades. Any answer built that way is a tautology dressed as a finding.

The version worth a week of work is: **conditional on the market reacting to out-year growth revisions at all, is the sensitivity stable, and is it the right size?** Concretely, three hypotheses, pre-committed in this order.

**H0 (feasibility gate, not a finding).** Consensus revenue and EBITDA two years out move enough, often enough, and for enough names, to support cross-sectional inference. If out-year estimates are stale placeholders that only ratchet at fiscal roll, there is nothing to study and we say so.

**H1 (primary).** The peer-relative sensitivity of re-rating to out-year growth revisions — call it β — is *not* stable across market regimes. Specifically, β is materially larger when discount-rate volatility is high, when the growth-minus-value spread is trending, and when cross-sectional revision dispersion is wide, and the variation exceeds what sampling noise alone would generate.

**H2 (primary).** The initial re-rating is not the efficient one. Either the market underreacts (revision-driven re-rating continues to accrue over the following one to twelve months) or it overreacts (it partially reverses), and we can say which by comparing β to a benchmark β\* calibrated from the *realized* persistence of the revised growth path.

H1 and H2 are the deliverable. The naive "does β > 0" question is demoted to a first-stage sanity check with a pre-committed pass threshold.

## Measurement choices that make the test non-circular

**The growth variable.** Do not use fiscal-year buckets, which jump mechanically at fiscal year-end. Build a constant-horizon 24-month-forward estimate by time-weighting FY1 and FY2 consensus by months remaining to each fiscal year-end (the standard interpolation), separately for revenue and EBITDA. The two-year log CAGR is then ½·[ln(E₂₄ᵐ) − ln(base)]. Because the base is a realized trailing figure that only updates at reporting, the *change* in the CAGR between reporting dates is exactly ½·Δln(E₂₄ᵐ). Use that as the revision variable, `ΔCAGR_rev` and `ΔCAGR_ebitda`, and never let a base update fall inside a measurement window.

**Same-analyst differencing is mandatory.** A large fraction of apparent consensus movement is composition: a bullish broker initiates, a stale bear drops out, the mean shifts and nothing was revised. Compute consensus changes on the fixed set of brokers that had a live estimate at both endpoints, from I/B/E/S (or Capital IQ) *detail* files, not the summary tape. Report side by side how much of raw consensus change survives this filter; if it is under half, that fact alone is a headline result.

**The multiple.** Primary dependent variable is Δln(EV / LTM EBITDA), where LTM EBITDA is the last *reported* four quarters as of the revision date, held fixed through the window. Because the denominator is an actual, not the estimate being revised, no part of the relationship is arithmetic. Over short windows this makes re-rating equal to the abnormal enterprise-value return, which is the honest statement of what "re-rating" means intraday; over six- and twelve-month windows the denominator updates and the decomposition Δln(EV) = Δln(LTM EBITDA) + Δln(multiple) becomes informative — that is where we learn whether the market paid for growth that showed up. Robustness: EV/LTM sales and P/LTM EPS. Never EV/NTM EBITDA as a primary.

**Peer-relative.** The question is about re-rating *among peers*, so everything is demeaned within GICS sub-industry × date, and the regressions carry industry×date fixed effects. Robustness on Hoberg–Phillips TNIC-3 peer sets and on FF48. Exclude financials and real estate (EBITDA is not meaningful), require ≥8 qualifying peers in the industry-month, ≥3 brokers with live FY2 revenue *and* EBITDA estimates, price > $5, market cap above the NYSE 20th percentile. Sample 2003–2025 (EBITDA estimate coverage is thin before that), US common shares.

## Breaking the simultaneity

The identifying problem is that analysts chase prices. Three layers of defence, in order of strength.

First, **event ordering at the broker level.** Define an event as a single broker's revision to its FY2 EBITDA (or revenue) estimate, timestamped by the I/B/E/S detail activation time, that moves the same-analyst consensus 24-month growth path by ≥50bp annualized. Measure abnormal returns from the *next* trading session onward for the announcement window if the activation time is after the close; use (−1,+1) as the primary window and always report (0,+1) alongside.

Second, **an off-cycle clean subsample as the main estimation sample.** Keep only events on days with no earnings release (Compustat RDQ ±2 days), no management guidance (I/B/E/S Guidance), no M&A announcement (SDC), no 8-K of a materially-price-relevant type, and no other broker revision on the same name that day. This costs perhaps 60–70% of events and is worth it: on earnings days the revision, the surprise, and the guidance are one bundled shock and β is unidentified. Estimate on the clean sample; report the earnings-day sample separately, with SUE controls, as a contrast rather than a pooled result.

Third, **an intraday ordering check on a subsample.** For 2010 onward, match detail activation timestamps to TAQ. Compute the cumulative signed return in the two hours *before* activation and the two hours after. If the pre-activation move is comparable to the post, the revision is following the price and the daily-frequency β is mostly reverse causality; we then reweight toward the clean subsample and disclose the leakage magnitude. This diagnostic is reported whether or not it is flattering.

## The pre-committed gate

Before any return regression, compute, per industry-month: the cross-sectional standard deviation of the trailing three-month same-analyst change in the 24-month EBITDA CAGR; the median absolute change; and the share of firm-months with any nonzero out-year revision in the prior 90 days. The gate passes for an industry if the SD ≥ 75bp annualized, median |Δ| ≥ 25bp, and ≥50% of firm-months show a live revision — in ≥60% of that industry's months. If fewer than half of industries pass, we do not run the pooled study; we run it only on passing industries and state the restriction on the front page. This is written down now so that a null on H1 cannot be laundered into "the market ignores growth" when the truth is "the estimates never moved."

The first-stage threshold is likewise fixed in advance: pooled announcement-window β must imply ≥20bp of peer-relative abnormal return per 100bp of annualized CAGR revision, with t > 5. Below that, H1 and H2 are underpowered and we report the gate result and stop.

## Estimation

**Baseline and stability.** Run monthly cross-sectional regressions of Δln(EV/LTM EBITDA) over the event window on standardized `ΔCAGR_ebitda` and `ΔCAGR_rev` jointly (they are correlated ~0.5; report both partials and the margin-revision component `ΔCAGR_ebitda − ΔCAGR_rev` as an alternative parameterization), with controls for size, book/market, momentum, prior-month revision, revision to FY1, analyst count, and broker fixed effects. Average the coefficients Fama–MacBeth style with Newey–West at 6 lags; also report a pooled panel with two-way clustering by firm and date, and confirm the two agree.

Take the resulting β_t series and test constancy three ways. A Wald test of equality across pre-committed regime buckets — VIX terciles from a trailing expanding distribution (no look-ahead), sign of the trailing six-month change in the 10-year real yield, terciles of the trailing twelve-month growth-minus-value return spread, terciles of cross-sectional revision dispersion, NFCI above/below zero — with p-values from a stationary block bootstrap over dates, mean block 12 months, 10,000 replications. A Bai–Perron sup-F for unknown breakpoints, 15% trimming, up to three breaks, HAC errors. And a variance-ratio: VR = Var(β̂_t) / mean(SE_t²). Pre-commit that we declare instability only if VR ≥ 2 with bootstrap p < 0.05 — a rolling β plot always looks unstable, and this is the discipline that stops us reading noise. Complement with a Kalman filter treating β as a random walk, to show a smoothed path with bands.

**Over- versus under-reaction.** Three tests that must agree before we make a claim.

(i) Drift. Regress CAR(+2,+63) and CAR(+64,+252) on the standardized event revision, industry-date demeaned, clustered two ways, with overlapping-window HAC. Mirror it with a calendar-time portfolio: within each industry, quintile-sort on the trailing revision, long top minus bottom, industry-neutral, held 3/6/12 months, and test the alpha against FF5 plus momentum. Require t > 3 given the number of specifications.

(ii) Persistence-implied benchmark. Estimate how much of a revision to the two-year path actually persists: regress realized years 3–5 growth (and the subsequent revisions to the two-year path) on the original revision. Feed the estimated persistence into a two-stage valuation identity — r = 9%, terminal growth 3%, constant reinvestment — to get β\*, the re-rating an efficient market should deliver per 100bp of revision. A revision that fully mean-reverts within two years is worth roughly the discounted 2-year cash-flow delta and almost no multiple change; a revision that persists into the terminal phase is worth a large multiple change. β ≫ β\* is overreaction; β ≪ β\* with positive drift is underreaction. Report β\* under a sensitivity grid over r and persistence, because this is the fragile step.

(iii) Persistence of the re-rating itself. Two-stage least squares: instrument the initial 0-to-+21-day change in ln(EV/LTM EBITDA) with the revision, and regress the +22-to-+252-day change in the same multiple on the fitted initial re-rating. A coefficient near zero means the re-rating sticks; near −1 means it fully unwinds. Split the unwind by whether realized growth subsequently confirmed the revision.

## Validation

Placebo events: shift every event date forward 30 calendar days and re-estimate; the announcement-window coefficient must vanish. Randomization: shuffle revision magnitudes within industry-month, 1,000 draws, and confirm the observed β lies outside the null distribution. Reverse-window diagnostic: run the announcement regression on CAR(−5,−2); a nonzero coefficient is expected (leakage) and its size is the honest discount we apply to the headline. Split-sample discipline: fix all regime definitions and specifications on 2003–2014, then run once on 2015–2025 and report the out-of-sample β and stability result without refitting. Multiplicity: Benjamini–Hochberg across the regime interactions, with a single pre-named primary regime variable (VIX terciles) that carries the headline. Power: report the minimum detectable β at the realized event count before interpreting any null.

## Pitfalls to watch

Composition-driven pseudo-revisions, addressed above, are the single most common way this study produces a fake result. Stale consensus is the second: use only estimates refreshed within 90 days when forming levels. Negative or near-zero LTM EBITDA makes the log multiple explode — screen at LTM EBITDA margin ≥ 2% and re-run on within-industry multiple *ranks* as a distribution-free check rather than winsorizing away the problem. Point-in-time discipline throughout: unrestated Compustat, contemporaneous GICS, split-unadjusted I/B/E/S detail with the adjustment factor applied as of the event date, CRSP delisting returns included. Enterprise value uses net debt from the most recently *filed* balance sheet, lagged by the actual filing date, not the period end. Expect asymmetry — negative revisions typically produce larger absolute reactions — so estimate separate coefficients for up- and down-revisions before pooling. Expect the rate regime to load mechanically, since duration alone makes growth revisions worth more when discount rates fall; separate that from sentiment by including the contemporaneous change in the 10-year real yield interacted with the firm's estimated duration, and report β conditional on it. Finally, resist the temptation to convert a non-rejection of stability into a claim of stability; that is what the variance ratio is for.
