# 7111ba

## The claim being tested

The question assumes the guarantee release is information. It may not be. Before anything else, the plan has to separate three worlds that produce the identical headline.

**H1 (the thesis as posed):** the hyperscaler negotiated its way out of credit support because it intends to reduce dependence on this manufacturer — it is qualifying an alternate source, it does not want its balance sheet standing behind capacity it may not fill, and it has enough leverage to make the manufacturer and the lenders wear the risk. Under H1 we should also see wafer/capacity commitments flattening, qualification activity elsewhere, and the manufacturer accepting worse terms because it needs the anchor tenant.

**H2 (mechanical release):** the guarantee was always a construction-period or ramp-period instrument that springs off on a pre-agreed test — completion certificate, commercial operation date, two consecutive quarters of a debt service coverage ratio above some threshold, the SPV achieving a standalone investment-grade shadow rating. Nothing was decided this year; the release was priced at signing. This is the modal outcome in sponsor-supported project paper and is the null hypothesis.

**H3 (substitution / accounting):** the guarantee came off but equivalent or greater economic risk went back on in another form — a hell-or-high-water offtake, a capped limited guarantee, a fronted letter of credit, a cash-funded reserve, a residual value guarantee on the tools, a contingent equity contribution agreement. The motive is consolidation (avoiding primary-beneficiary status under ASC 810 by shedding the obligation-to-absorb-losses prong), rating-agency adjusted-debt treatment, or keeping the number out of the purchase obligations table. Optically de-risked, economically unchanged or worse.

H1 is only interesting if H2 and H3 are affirmatively excluded. The work below is organized so that the cheapest disconfirming evidence comes first.

## Week one: the paper, in order

Start with the release instrument itself, then work backwards to the document it amends, then outward.

Pull the SPV's formation stack from the Delaware Division of Corporations: certificate of formation, and — where available through the registered agent or the financing exhibits — the limited liability company agreement and any amended and restated version. What matters is the member schedule, the capital commitment schedule, the class structure (whether the hyperscaler holds a non-voting or tracking interest), the kick-out and participating rights, and the transfer/put-call provisions. Consolidation analysis turns on these, and so does the question of who actually controls the asset.

Then the financing. Depending on structure this is a syndicated credit agreement, a note purchase agreement for a 4(a)(2) private placement, or a 144A offering with an indenture and an offering memorandum. Get the offering memorandum if it exists — the "Description of the Notes," "Summary of Principal Financing Documents," and "Risk Factors" sections restate the guarantee package in plain language and are the fastest route into a 300-page credit agreement. Then find, by name: the Guarantee Agreement (or the guaranty article of the credit agreement); the Sponsor Support Agreement; the Equity Contribution Agreement; any Completion Guarantee, Contingent Equity Support Agreement, or keepwell. In the credit agreement, the clauses that decide H1 versus H2 are the guarantor release conditions and the definition of Project Completion / Commercial Operation Date, plus the DSCR definition and the reserve account covenants. Read the release condition against the original execution version, not the conformed copy: if the release language is present in the as-signed document, H2 is live and the "news" is a certificate delivery.

Then the amendment trail. Every amendment, consent, and waiver — numbered, dated. Note the amendment fee (a consent fee of a few basis points paid to lenders tells you the release was *requested*, not automatic), any change to the applicable margin grid, any new financial covenant, any newly funded reserve, and any change to the collateral package. An automatic release does not require lender consent and does not pay a fee. That single fact — was there a consent solicitation — is close to dispositive between H1/H3 and H2, and it is cheap to establish.

Then the commercial contract. The master supply or wafer supply agreement, the capacity reservation agreement, and every amendment. The line items to extract: annual minimum volumes and the take-or-pay percentage; the capacity corridor and the notice period to flex it; prepayments, advance deposits, and the conditions under which deposits are refundable or forfeited; capex reimbursement and tool ownership (consigned versus owned versus SPV-owned); pricing schedule and any most-favored-nation or exclusivity provision; qualification and yield milestones; termination for convenience and the cancellation ladder; change-of-control and assignment. If the exhibit is filed with redactions under Item 601(b)(10)(iv), reconstruct the redacted quanta from the counterparty's disclosures and from the SPV's own debt sizing — lenders size to contracted cash flow, so the debt quantum plus the assumed DSCR back-solves an approximate contracted revenue line.

Then the filings, both sides. On the hyperscaler: the 8-K Item 1.01 and Item 2.03 around the change; the guarantees note (ASC 460); the variable interest entity note and specifically the maximum exposure to loss line for unconsolidated VIEs; off-balance-sheet arrangements in MD&A; the purchase obligations table and its by-year buckets; finance lease and financing obligation notes; related-party transactions. Track the quarter-over-quarter delta in maximum exposure to loss — that is the company's own auditor-reviewed number for how much risk left, and it is the cleanest single test of H3. Also pull any SEC comment letter correspondence on VIE consolidation; staff questions are where the real structuring rationale gets written down. On the manufacturer, if foreign-domiciled, use the 20-F or local annual report, the material-transaction announcements on the local exchange system, board-approved capital appropriation disclosures, and the balance sheet lines for advance receipts from customers, long-term contract liabilities, refundable deposits, and guarantee deposits received. A rising customer-prepayment balance concurrent with a guarantee release is substitution, not de-risking.

Finally the perimeter: UCC-1 financing statements in Delaware for the collateral description and any new secured party; county fixture filings and mortgages at the site; rating agency new-issue and presale reports for the SPV, which will state explicitly how much uplift the parent guarantee provided and what the standalone assessment is; and any subsequent rating action citing the release.

## Sizing the transfer

Build a two-column bridge, pre and post, in notional and in expected loss.

Notional side: guaranteed principal; whether the guarantee was of payment or of collection; whether it was full or capped, and at what cap; whether it covered interest, breakage, and the swap mark-to-market as well as principal; the completion guarantee; the unfunded equity contribution obligation; any residual value guarantee on equipment. Post-release: the cap on any replacement limited guarantee; the face of any letter of credit; months of debt service in the funded reserve; the NPV of any new or strengthened take-or-pay at the SPV's discount rate.

Expected loss side is where the analysis earns its keep, and it is the step most people skip. Notional moved is not risk moved. Take the SPV's standalone rating (from the presale report, or shadow it off the DSCR and contract quality), map to a through-the-cycle default probability, apply a loss-given-default consistent with a single-purpose asset with narrow alternative use — advanced-node fab tooling and purpose-built shells have punishing LGD — and compute the expected loss transferred. Then compare that to the incremental spread the SPV paid at the release: if lenders demanded, say, 60–90bp and a funded reserve, they priced real risk moving; if the paper barely moved, the guarantee was not doing much work and the release is not the signal the headline implies. Cross-check against the SPV note spread versus the hyperscaler's own curve at the same tenor, and against CDS on both names around the announcement date.

## The second-source leg

Documentary evidence of a guarantee release tells you about risk allocation. It does not tell you about sourcing. Those need independent corroboration, and the timelines have to line up: qualifying an alternate advanced-node source is an 18–36 month program, so if H1 is true, qualification activity should *precede* the financing change, not follow it.

Look for: alternate-foundry PDK and IP licensing for the same design family (EDA and IP vendors' customer disclosures, conference papers at IEDM, ISSCC, VLSI, Hot Chips naming process nodes); advanced packaging diversification away from the incumbent's proprietary flow toward OSAT or rival integrated packaging, visible in substrate and OSAT order patterns; HBM vendor requalification; test and burn-in capacity bookings; job postings for foundry engagement, PDK enablement, and second-source qualification roles, with location tells; government incentive award recipients and named foundry customers; import/export bill-of-lading and national trade statistics for the relevant HS lines and origin shifts.

Channel work, with questions specific enough to be answerable. To project finance counsel not on the deal: in this vintage of paper, is sponsor release at completion or at a DSCR test standard, and what typically substitutes — reserve, LC, or offtake? To a lender or the syndicate desk: was there a consent solicitation, what was the fee, did the margin grid change, did anyone sell into secondary on the news? To former supply-chain planners at the hyperscaler: has the forward-year allocation number moved, and has anyone been asked to dual-qualify packaging or substrates? To OSATs and substrate suppliers: have you been asked to quote a non-incumbent packaging flow for this part family? To EDA and IP account teams: has this customer taken the alternate foundry's PDK for this design family, and when? To the rival foundry's account side: did an RFQ arrive, for what node, and for what wafer volume? Require two independent sources at different nodes of the chain before treating any of this as established.

## Validation and falsification

Write down, before the channel work, what would kill the thesis. The killers are: release language present verbatim in the as-signed credit agreement with an objective test that was in fact met; no consent solicitation and no fee; the maximum-exposure-to-loss disclosure falling by less than the released notional because a new instrument replaced it; contracted minimum volumes flat or rising in the amended supply agreement; no alternate-foundry qualification activity dated before the financing change. Any two of those and the answer to the question is no.

Establish a base rate rather than reasoning from one document: assemble five to ten comparable sponsor-supported SPVs of the same vintage, ideally sharing lead arrangers or counsel, and check how often the sponsor guarantee falls away at completion. If it is the norm, the prior on H2 should be high and the evidentiary bar for H1 correspondingly high.

## Pitfalls to hold in mind

Redactions will hide exactly the numbers that matter; back-solve rather than guess. A hyperscaler can add a second source and still grow with the incumbent — dual sourcing is a thesis about the incumbent's *pricing power*, not necessarily its volumes, and the trade expression must respect that distinction. Non-commercial motives are common and confounding: antitrust or joint-control avoidance, foreign investment review, export-control attribution, transfer pricing on guarantee fees, or thin-capitalization rules can all drive a guarantee off the page. Beware treating a single 8-K as new information when the market moved on the day and the trade is already in the price — check the price reaction before committing risk. And beware the analyst's version of confirmation bias here: once you have read a 300-page credit agreement, every clause starts to look intentional. Most of them are precedent.

## Output

A written bridge of risk pre and post, in notional and expected loss; a dated timeline placing the guarantee release against qualification evidence; a verdict on H1 versus H2 versus H3 with the specific documents cited; and, if H1 survives, a trade expression sized to the weakest link in the chain — which will almost always be the channel corroboration, not the documents.
