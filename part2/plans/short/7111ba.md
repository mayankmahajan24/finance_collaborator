**H1:** reduced dependence — alternate source qualifying, refusal to back capacity it may not fill, leverage pushing risk onto manufacturer and lenders; expect flat capacity commitments, qualification elsewhere, worse terms to keep the tenant. **H2 (null; modal in sponsor-supported paper):** construction/ramp support springing off a pre-agreed test — completion certificate, COD, two quarters DSCR above threshold, standalone IG shadow rating. **H3:** risk back on as hell-or-high-water offtake, capped guarantee, fronted LC, funded reserve, tool RVG, contingent equity — dodging ASC 810 primary-beneficiary status (obligation-to-absorb-losses prong), rating-agency adjusted debt, purchase-obligations table. H1 only if H2/H3 die, cheapest disconfirmation first.

Documents. Delaware: formation certificate, LLC agreement + A&R — members, capital commitments, class structure (non-voting/tracking), kick-out/participating rights, transfer/put-call. Hyperscaler: 8-K 1.01/2.03, ASC 460 note, VIE maximum-exposure-to-loss QoQ delta (best H3 test), off-balance-sheet MD&A, purchase obligations by year, finance-lease/financing-obligation notes, related-party, SEC VIE-consolidation comment letters. Validation. Killers: release language verbatim as-signed, test met; no consent solicitation or fee; exposure-to-loss drop below released notional; volumes flat or rising; no qualification predating the change. Any two, no. Base rate: 5–10 same-vintage sponsor-supported SPVs, shared arrangers/counsel.

Pitfalls. Redactions hide the key numbers — back-solve. A second source can coexist with incumbent growth: dual sourcing is about pricing power, not volumes — trade accordingly. Confounders — antitrust/joint-control avoidance, foreign-investment review, export-control attribution, guarantee-fee transfer pricing, thin capitalization. Check the price reaction first. After 300 pages every clause looks intentional; most are precedent.
