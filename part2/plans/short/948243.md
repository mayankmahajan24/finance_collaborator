# 948243

The stated question — are round-lot orders retail flow profitable to trade against — bundles two claims: (1) round-lot size predicts retail origination, (2) trading against isolated retail flow earns money after costs. Most versions run these together, backtest an imbalance signal, and retrofit a retail story if it looks good — backwards, since round-lot imbalance can predict returns mechanically via order-flow imbalance with no retail content. This project tests claim (1) only, with a hard stop before any signal is built.

**H1:** round-lot size predicts retail origination with ≥2x lift over unconditional retail share, capturing ≥1/3 of retail volume, with above-chance signing accuracy. **H0 (my prior):** round lots are uninformative once venue is controlled for, because retail has moved to dollar-denominated/fractional orders (arriving as odd/mixed lots) while institutional VWAP/TWAP schedulers slice into 100-share children — making round lots closer to an institutional signature. If H0 holds, the deliverable is a well-evidenced negative result plus a map of better retail identifiers.

**Benchmarking (no ground truth exists; triangulate imperfect labels).** A — sub-penny price improvement on TRF prints (z = 100P − floor(100P); 0<z<0.4 retail sell, 0.6<z<1 retail buy): high precision, low recall, sign-error rates in the tens of percent, and broken by migration to midpoint pricing and the half-penny tick regime, so valid only pre-half-penny-compliance. B — exchange Retail Liquidity Program flags (NYSE, NYSE American, Arca, Cboe EDGX/BYX, Nasdaq, MEMX, IEX): near-1.0 precision by RMO certification, very low recall; must confirm which feeds carry the flag in historical (not just live) data. C — Rule 605 wholesaler reports (Citadel Securities, Virtu, G1X, Two Sigma, Jane Street, UBS), broken out by odd-lot/fractional/dollar buckets, paired with FINRA OTC transparency by MPID — cheap, spreadsheet-only, run first since it alone can kill the project. D (optional) — VandaTrack daily net retail buying as external confirmation.

**Data:** CRSP codes 10/11, NYSE/Nasdaq/NYSE American primary listing, $3–$600, ≥250k median daily volume (~2,000–2,500 names, size quintiles + $250+ bucket). 36 months ending before the half-penny compliance date. NYSE Daily TAQ trades + NBBO (nanosecond, full sale conditions), partitioned Parquet, DuckDB/Spark; exchange proprietary feeds for RLP flags; CRSP for returns. Compute round-lot status under both fixed-100 and the new tiered definition (100/40/10/1 shares by price tier). Sign lit trades via Lee-Ready, 1ms lag, checking robustness to zero-lag/tick test.

**Method:** partition by venue first (off-exchange TRF; lit w/ RLP; lit w/o RLP; off-exchange further split by NBBO position and sub-penny improvement) — never pool. Decompose round lots into exactly-100, multiples-of-100, and mixed lots. Five gate tests: G1 precision/lift (Rule 605 accounting + Hui-Walter latent-class estimate; kill if lift <2.0x or bootstrap lower bound <1.5x); G2 recall (kill <35%); G3 signing accuracy vs RLP-flagged known-side executions (kill <70%, hard stop <60%); G4 stock-day correlation vs Benchmark A (kill mean r<0.40) and vs D (kill <0.25); G5 panel regression of forward returns on Benchmark A imbalance + round-lot imbalance, double-clustered SEs (kill |t|<3). Plus two mechanism tests: price-elasticity of round-lot frequency (negative for RLP-flagged retail, flat for schedulers) and round-notional clustering ($100/$500/$1,000/$5,000) vs round-share clustering. Proceed to Phase 2 only if G1-G5 all pass in one partition. Phase 2 (conditional): build imbalance signal, test directional continuation (decile sorts, Fama-MacBeth) and liquidity-provision economics (spread decomposition); go/no-go at after-cost Sharpe ≥1.0, no negative year, net/gross >0.40.

**Pitfalls:** meme-stock window distorting the panel; stock splits; pre-2013 absence of odd lots from the tape; tiered round-lot and half-penny structural breaks; noisy-label understatement of agreement (motivating latent-class); multiple testing across partitions/benchmarks/horizons (pre-register thresholds, Benjamini-Hochberg); inconsistent fractional-share reporting across wholesalers; secular drift of "retail" toward systematic day-trading, eroding the premise even if identification succeeds.
