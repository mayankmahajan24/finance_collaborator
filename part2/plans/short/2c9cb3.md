**Methodology.** Code 40–60 comparable releases, three strata: semiconductor capacity JVs/fab SPVs; digital-infrastructure SPVs (data center JVs, private-credit build vehicles with residual value guarantees, data center ABS/CMBS); classic project finance (power, LNG, toll). Portfolio sweep: eight quarters of guarantee inventory — guarantees/indemnifications footnote, off-balance-sheet arrangements, purchase obligations table, every SPV/lease vehicle/JV in the 10-K; withdrawals across unrelated vehicles → treasury policy, plus CFO capital-efficiency language. Pricing: SPV spread pre/post release (TRACE, dealer runs, secondary marks) × duration × notional, versus release fee, wafer pricing, equity injection, take-or-pay extension, volume floor.

**Falsifiers, pre-registered.** Condition in signing documents, met on schedule → no signal. Parent replaced by subsidiary guarantor, keepwell, ECL → observation wrong, stop. Guarantees withdrawn across unrelated vehicles in window → treasury policy. Incumbent committed spend rose, or reservation/prepayment within two quarters either way → dependence rising. No independent qualification within twelve months, no available advanced packaging → H1 lacks mechanism. Deconsolidation entry, VIE footnote change at release, CFO commentary → H0-b sufficient. Take-or-pay, volume minimum, residual value guarantee replacing it → risk transfer falsified. First three → confident no.

**Pitfalls.** Legal form vs economics: guarantee, indemnity, keepwell, ECL, residual value guarantee, take-or-pay not interchangeable. Anchoring on the strategic story via noisy hiring/IP evidence. Option vs decision: cheap qualification supports buying leverage, not switching. Timing illusion: foundry decisions precede silicon by two to three years; arrow may run backwards. Press-sampled comparables inflate the strategic base rate; sample filings/databases. H3 externally identical to H1, opposite implications; separate with demand-side evidence, not financing documents.
