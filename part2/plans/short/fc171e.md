# fc171e

Management turned evasive/scripted on a vendor licence renegotiation. Tone in a live negotiation is confounded by the negotiation itself — a CFO sounds the same either way — so it earns no weight. Substitute question: margin at stake per unit of price increase, weighted by likelihood, given contract mechanics, vendor leverage, and vendor behavior toward comparables and this customer. Outcome under ~25bps makes tone moot; central case over ~75bps and unguided forces a call.

**Hypothesis:** licence is mid-single-digit share of opex; bad case is 15–30% uplift phased over the term; margin hit 30–90bps — material to a quarter, not the thesis — unless single-source with a step-function change (per-server-to-per-core metric shift, or a bundling/audit settlement), where the tail is several times larger.

**Step 1 — Size the spend.** Top-down and bottom-up, reconciled within ~20%. Top-down: 10-K purchase-obligations table, supplier-concentration note, ASC 350-40 cloud disclosures, lease notes, MD&A commentary (5yr trend). Bottom-up: licensing metric (seats, cores, sockets, consumption units) × count driver × vendor list price (GSA/Crown Commercial/state schedules give negotiated pricing), haircut for discount tier. Cross-check: vendor 10-K mentions, RFP postings, LinkedIn headcount, audit settlements. Output: annual spend, % of COGS/opex/revenue/EBIT, capitalized vs. expensed split (ASC 985-20/350-40 amortization lags cash).

**Step 2 — Contract mechanics.** Renewal date/term; evergreen vs. re-signature; price-protection cap (CPI-linked or 3–5% ceiling) and its survival into the new term; true-up/true-down rights; metric changes (highest priority — where 40%+ uplifts hide behind flat headline price); audit clauses; termination rights; MFN clauses; support pricing (~20–22% of licence value; often the outlet when licence price caps). Sources: material-contracts exhibits, risk factors, vendor price list, prior 8-Ks.

**Step 3 — Vendor's pricing behavior.** Price-list changes (3yr), net revenue retention/expansion rate, RPO vs. billings growth (separates volume from price), earnings-call commentary; triangulate with Gartner/Forrester and SAM benchmarks. Comparable set of 5–10 similar customers renewed in 24 months; observed uplift as a distribution (median/75th/90th percentile). Switching cost: migration (integration points × cost/point + retraining + parallel-run licensing) vs. price-delta NPV; if migration NPV exceeds three years of the increase, assume vendor prices near the top of its distribution.

**Step 4 — This pair's history.** Reconstruct the last 2–3 renewals — date, term, expense-line inflection over the next four quarters, and what management said vs. what happened — calibrating reassurance better than tone.

**Step 5 — Sensitivity table.** Axes: uplift (0/5/10/15/20/30/50%) × phasing (immediate; half-year; three-year ramp); cells: dollars, margin bps, EPS impact, consensus impact. Margin bps = (spend × uplift × share landing in period × (1 − share capitalized)) ÷ revenue × 10,000; EPS = dollars × (1 − tax rate) ÷ diluted shares. Add a metric-change scenario (unit-count change). Invert: solve required uplift to (a) breach guided margin floor, (b) miss consensus EPS by 2%, (c) breach covenant headroom; compare to Step 3's distribution. Net against offsets: pass-through escalators, bundled savings, capitalization shifts.

**Validation:** back-test against the prior renewal's known inputs, checking it predicts the realized move within error bars; top-down/bottom-up reconcile within 20%; adversarial review arguing the licence is half the assumed size; sanity-check implied spend doesn't exceed ~15% of the tech cost base.

**Pitfalls:** tone sneaking in as an upward nudge; confusing gross list-to-list uplift with net realized uplift after discount changes; missing metric changes that hide price increases; timing errors between cash, expense, and amortization; double-counting against uplift embedded in guidance; survivorship bias (unpublicized bad deals — treat the 75th percentile as median for high-switching-cost customers); over-reading a single quarter (require four post-renewal quarters); and concluding smallness means nothing is wrong: if immateriality is shown but discomfort persists, log a separate open question.
