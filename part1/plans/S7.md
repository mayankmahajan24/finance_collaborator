# S7 — plan (claude-code subagent)

> Sell-side channel checks are incrementally positive on new logo wins. Should we expect a meaningful boost to full-year guidance at the upcoming print, and is this a long?

## What the idea actually asks, and why it has to be split

As stated, this is two questions wearing one coat. The first is an event-prediction question: will management raise the full-year guide by more than the mechanical flow-through of a quarterly beat? The second is a return question: does owning the stock into and through that event make money? These do not answer each other. A correct call on the raise loses money if the raise is already in consensus, in the buy-side bar, and in the price; an incorrect call on the raise can still make money if positioning is washed out. The plan below runs them as two tracks that share a data spine but have separate tests, separate falsifiers, and separate decision rules.

A third, quieter question is buried in the phrase "is this a long," which implies a holding period of quarters. Channel checks are a catalyst signal with a half-life of days. If the analyst wants a multi-quarter position, the channel-check work is at best the timing overlay on a thesis that must stand on its own economics. I would make the researcher declare the intended horizon on Monday morning before any data work, because it determines which track gets the effort.

Throughout, "the name" refers to the single ticker in question, which the analyst should pin in the first hour along with fiscal calendar, print date, and the exact prior FY guide language from the last 8-K exhibit. I assume an enterprise software or subscription-hardware business, since "new logo" is that vocabulary; if the name is transactional or consumption-priced, the flow-through arithmetic in Step 1 changes and I flag the substitution below.

## Hypothesis, stated so it can fail

H1 (event): Conditional on a cluster of net-positive sell-side channel checks on new logo acquisition published in the six weeks before the print, the company raises its FY revenue guidance midpoint by more than the quarter's revenue beat — that is, the incremental raise, defined as Δ(FY guide midpoint) minus (reported quarterly revenue minus consensus quarterly revenue), is positive and exceeds 0.5% of the prior FY guide midpoint.

H2 (trade): That event, conditional on the pre-print setup, produces positive risk-adjusted return over the [0, +1] day window after hedging sector beta.

H0 for both: positive channel checks carry no information beyond what is already in point-in-time consensus and price; the incremental-raise rate conditional on positive checks equals the unconditional base rate; post-event abnormal return is zero once you condition on pre-print drift.

The definition of "meaningful" is the load-bearing choice here, and I am making it deliberately strict. Most FY raises in software are not news — they are last quarter's beat pushed into the annual number with the back half left untouched. The flow-through ratio, Δ FY guide ÷ Q beat, is the diagnostic: below 1.0x management is banking cushion, at 1.0x it is pure mechanics, above 1.0x management is genuinely marking up the forward. Only above 1.0x, and only when the excess is at least 0.5% of the FY guide, does the channel-check story get credit. I would also require the raise to survive an FX adjustment, since a weak-dollar quarter can manufacture the entire delta on a company with 30%+ international revenue.

## Step 1, Monday morning: the arithmetic that kills most of these ideas

Before any subscription is queried or any panel is built, size the claim. Take the checks at face value and ask what they are worth in dollars of in-quarter revenue. If the checks imply, say, thirty incremental new logos at a $150k average first-year ACV, that is $4.5M of ARR signed at an average of mid-quarter, which on ratable recognition is roughly $0.5–0.8M of in-quarter revenue. Against a $400M quarter that is fifteen basis points. Against a $1.6B FY guide, even a full-year annualization is under 30bps — well below the 0.5% threshold in H1.

This is the single highest-value hour in the week and it is done from the 10-K, the last four 8-K exhibits, and a legal pad. Three inputs are needed: average new-logo ACV (from disclosed customer counts and revenue, or from management's own "customers over $100k ARR" cohort disclosure), the share of net new ARR that comes from new logos versus expansion, and the revenue recognition lag from signature to P&L. For most mature enterprise software companies, expansion within existing accounts is 70–90% of net new ARR. A signal that is entirely about new logos is therefore levered to 10–30% of the growth engine, and a large move in that slice produces a small move in the aggregate. If the arithmetic cannot get to 0.5% of the FY guide under generous assumptions, the idea is dead as stated and the analyst should stop and reframe — either the checks are actually telling you something about pricing, churn, or deal cycle length rather than logo count, or the trade is about narrative rather than numbers, which is a different and shorter trade.

Kill criterion: if the generous-case incremental revenue is under 0.25% of the FY guide, write it up in a page and move on.

## Step 2: the guidance ledger

Build a point-in-time ledger of the last sixteen to twenty quarters for the name. Sources are the 8-K Item 2.02 press-release exhibits pulled from EDGAR full-text search, the XBRL company-facts API for reported actuals, and the earnings-call transcripts from S&P Capital IQ or AlphaSense for the verbal guide (management frequently gives the real number in the prepared remarks and only the range in the release). Columns: prior FY guide low/mid/high, consensus for the quarter as of the day before the print, reported quarter, new FY guide low/mid/high, implied back-half guide, the flow-through ratio, FX contribution as disclosed, any M&A contribution, and a flag for guidance-policy changes.

That ledger gives you the firm's own conditional base rate, which is more informative than any cross-sectional panel. Companies have persistent guidance philosophies. A CFO who has flowed through 0.6x for eight straight quarters is not going to flow through 1.4x because a VAR told a broker things feel good. Watch specifically for CFO turnover and for the transition from Q2 to Q3 prints — Q1 and Q2 raises are systematically stingier because management preserves cushion, and Q3 raises are near-mechanical because the year is nearly done. Conditioning on fiscal quarter is not optional.

## Step 3: what is already priced

Assemble the expectations map. Consensus revenue and EPS for the quarter and the FY from I/B/E/S, using the unadjusted detail history file so that analyst-level revisions are visible and split adjustments do not corrupt levels. Compute the revision trajectory over the last thirty days: if three or more analysts have already raised FY numbers above the company guide, the sell-side has pre-baked the raise and the surprise is gone. Add Estimize or the fund's own broker-desk color for the buy-side bar, which typically sits above published consensus by 100–200bps of revenue growth in a name with a positive-check narrative.

Then price: cumulative excess return over [-20, -1] versus a sector benchmark (IGV or XLK for software, or a tight peer basket of six to ten names — I prefer the peer basket because sector ETFs are index-heavy and will not capture a mid-cap's factor exposure). Options: the at-the-money straddle for the first expiry after the print gives the implied move; compare it to the realized absolute [0,+1] move for the last twelve prints. Short interest and borrow fee from C1 or the prime broker's stock-loan desk. Crowding matters: if the name has outperformed its peer basket by more than 5% over the prior month and short interest has fallen, the channel-check information has already been disseminated and monetized, and the trade is now a bet on a raise larger than a market that already expects a raise.

## Step 4: base rates from comparable episodes

You cannot backtest N=1, so build a modest panel to calibrate. Universe: 60–100 US-listed software and subscription names with market cap over $1bn and continuous FY revenue guidance, over the last five to seven years, giving roughly 1,500–2,500 firm-quarters. For each, compute the incremental-raise measure from Step 2's definitions.

The treatment variable — "sell-side published net-positive channel checks on new logos in the six weeks before the print" — is the hard part and the place where the plan is most likely to break. There is no clean vendor field for this. Two approaches, run together. First, hand-code from the fund's own broker note archive: search AlphaSense or the entitled research inbox for "channel check," "partner survey," "VAR check," "reseller check," "field work" scoped to the universe and the pre-print window, and code each note as positive/neutral/negative on new-logo demand specifically, with the publication timestamp preserved. Budget 150–250 hand-coded episodes; that is one analyst-week and it is the honest cost of this idea. Second, a proxy that scales: pre-print upward revisions to the *current-quarter* estimate by two or more analysts in the absence of any company disclosure or guidance event in the window, which is the observable footprint that check-driven notes leave. Report results under both; if they disagree, trust the hand-coded set and treat the proxy as invalid.

Estimation should stay deliberately small. With a few hundred treated episodes, the right answer is a conditional rate with a Jeffreys interval, not a model: P(incremental raise > 0.5% | positive checks) versus the unconditional base rate, stratified by fiscal quarter and by the firm's trailing four-quarter flow-through regime. If a regression is wanted, a Firth-penalized logit with at most four covariates — positive-check flag, fiscal-quarter dummies, trailing flow-through ratio, and pre-print excess return — avoids separation and keeps the degrees of freedom honest. Cluster standard errors by firm and by calendar quarter, because check-cycles and guidance-cycles are both strongly correlated across the sector in the same quarter. Do not reach for gradient boosting on 200 events.

For H2, an event study: abnormal return over [0, +1] measured as residual to a Fama-French five-factor plus momentum model estimated on [-250, -30], and separately as a simple peer-basket hedge, because factor models fit mid-cap software badly. The regression that answers the actual question is CAR ~ incremental_raise + pre_print_excess_return + their interaction. If the interaction is negative and significant, the market pre-pays for positive checks and the trade is to fade crowded setups, not to follow them.

## Step 5: independent corroboration of the checks

Channel checks are testimony; look for evidence. Concretely, for this name: customer-logo page snapshots from the Wayback Machine at monthly intervals to count net new named logos; job postings from Revelio or LinkUp filtered to implementation, onboarding, professional services, and customer success roles, which lead recognized revenue on newly signed accounts by one to two quarters; technographic installs from HG Insights or BuiltWith if the product leaves a detectable footprint; G2 and Gartner Peer Insights new-review counts, which correlate with new-customer cohorts; public-sector contract awards from USASpending/FPDS, UK Contracts Finder, and TED if government is a meaningful vertical; and cloud-marketplace listings (AWS, Azure, GCP) where private-offer volume has become a real channel. Also read the last two quarters of transcripts for what management said about *deal scrutiny and cycle length* — if the CFO has been flagging elongating cycles, a rise in logo count at smaller land sizes is consistent with weaker, not stronger, revenue.

None of these will be conclusive alone. The test is directional agreement: if three of five independent proxies point the same way as the checks, raise confidence; if the checks are the only positive signal, treat them as a broker's marketing document.

## Validation

Pre-register. Before the print, the analyst writes a timestamped file containing the numeric prediction for the FY guide midpoint, the predicted flow-through ratio, the probability assigned to H1, the expected [0,+1] move, and the specific observations that would falsify the thesis. This is what makes the exercise cumulative rather than a story told after the fact; without it, the post-mortem will rationalize whatever happened.

Beyond that: hold out the most recent eight quarters of the panel from any specification search and check the base rate out of sample. Double-code a random fifty-episode subsample of the channel-check labels with a second reader and require Cohen's kappa above 0.7; if the labels are not reproducible, the treatment variable does not exist. Run the panel with and without calendar 2020–2021, which is a regime that will otherwise dominate any software guidance study and inflate every raise rate. Run a placebo: notes containing "channel check" language about topics other than new logos (pricing, competitive displacement, macro) should show no incremental-raise lift; if they show the same lift, what you have measured is broker optimism generally, not logo information. Finally, sanity-check the money: convert the estimated edge into an expected return per event and compare it to round-trip cost plus the option-implied move. An edge of 40bps on a name with a 7% implied move is noise.

## Pitfalls I would watch for

Selection in the checks themselves is the deepest problem. Distribution partners agree to talk when business is good and go quiet when it is not, and brokers publish checks when they have something to say. The observed sample of positive checks is therefore not a random draw from the state of demand. Related, correlated sourcing: three brokers publishing "independent" checks may have surveyed the same handful of VARs. Audit named or described sources across notes and collapse overlapping checks into a single observation, or the effective sample size is a fraction of the note count.

Partner-channel enthusiasm can also mean the wrong thing. Heavy channel activity can reflect discounting, quarter-end incentive stuffing, or a partner-margin promotion — mechanisms that raise logo counts while lowering realized ACV and gross margin. Check whether the checks discuss discount levels; if they do not, that is itself informative.

On the data side, the classic errors: using restated consensus rather than point-in-time, which makes historical beats look larger than they were; ignoring segment reorganizations that break guidance comparability; missing that the company changed its guidance policy (dropped FY revenue guidance, moved to cRPO, moved to constant-currency-only); and treating the press-release range midpoint as the guide when management verbally steered to a different number on the call. Also note that "beat" against a guide the company itself sandbagged is not a demand signal.

On inference: with a few hundred events, running twenty specifications will produce a significant one. Fix the specification before looking. And be alert to the horizon mismatch already mentioned — an event-conditional edge that exists over two days is not an argument for a position held to the next print.

Finally, compliance. Channel checks brush against expert-network and MNPI territory. Use only firm-approved research providers, log the notes and their timestamps, and escalate to compliance if any note contains what reads as specific undisclosed financial data from an employee of the issuer. That is not a formality; it is the fastest way this idea becomes a problem rather than a position.

## Decision rule and deliverable

The analyst returns Friday with a one-page recommendation that either takes the trade or declines it against explicit conditions. I would go long only if all four hold: the Step 1 arithmetic supports at least 0.5% of FY revenue upside under central (not generous) assumptions; the estimated P(incremental raise) exceeds the firm-specific base rate by at least 15 percentage points; pre-print excess return versus the peer basket is under +4%, so the setup is not crowded; and the option-implied move is at or below the trailing median realized move, so the event is not already expensive. If the first three hold but implied volatility is rich, express it in the stock rather than options; if the arithmetic is thin but positioning is washed out, that is a short-horizon positioning trade sized at a third of a normal event position and closed within two days of the print, not a long. If the arithmetic fails, the answer to the researcher's second question is no regardless of what happens to the guide, and the write-up should say so plainly.
