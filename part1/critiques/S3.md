# S3 — critique (claude-opus-5)

> Can we reliably identify European financial institutions from transaction data, using only anonymized institution IDs plus the demographics and spend behavior of a panel of customers?

---

## Bottom line

The craft here is good — better than most of what crosses this desk. The person who wrote this has actually looked at European transaction data: the consent-asymmetry point, the processor-confusion warning, the fixed-cohort YoY discipline, and the refusal to use panel share as market share are all things people learn the hard way. The methodology section is over-engineered but not naive.

The problem is that the plan never does the arithmetic that determines whether any of it is worth doing, and it has a structural contradiction at the top that dissolves the project either way. As scoped — 13 weeks, 700 candidates, 200 features — I would not fund this. I would fund a five-week gated feasibility study with three hard stops, described at the end.

---

## The three things that kill it

**1. The legal gate contains its own refutation.** Section 2 says most European transaction contracts prohibit re-identification, and that "the practical resolution is usually commercial: ask the vendor to sell a named financial-counterparty feed." If the vendor will sell that feed, the entire modelling project is unnecessary — you buy the answer for less than thirteen analyst-weeks. If the vendor won't sell it, that refusal is almost certainly the reason the field is masked, and building the model anyway is a contract breach you will have to disclose in every operational due diligence you face for the next decade. The narrow surviving case is a vendor who is indifferent and strips the field for engineering or GDPR-hygiene reasons rather than contractual ones. That case exists, but you establish it with two phone calls in week zero, not with a research plan. Note also that asking the vendor for permission on a defined list tells them exactly which institutions you care about.

**2. Nobody counted the tradeable names.** The plan's own "graceful retreat" — neobanks, brokers, BNPL in UK/DE/ES — is where the identification signal actually is, for exactly the reasons the plan gives. Now look at the fintech list in Section 3: Revolut, N26, bunq, Monzo, Starling, Trade Republic, Scalable, Trading 212, Zopa, Younited, Alma, Scalapay, Wise, Klarna, eToro. Check the current status of each yourself, but as of writing the overwhelming majority are private, and the two most prominent that have listed (Klarna, eToro) listed in the US, not Europe. You are proposing to build a European identification engine whose highest-confidence output is a set of companies you cannot trade. Meanwhile the segment that *is* listed and liquid — incumbent universal and mid-cap banks — is where the fingerprints are weakest, the twins problem is worst, and, per point 3 below, the panel physically cannot see the relevant flows.

Do this on paper in week one: enumerate European-listed financials where (a) between-quarter KPI visibility is genuinely poor, (b) the stock moves on that KPI, (c) the retail flow footprint is visible in a bank-transaction panel, and (d) there is no indistinguishable peer. My prior is you land somewhere between ten and twenty-five names. Then price what a mediocre-precision quarterly nowcast on twenty names is worth. If that number doesn't clear the cost of the panel plus a quarter of analyst time plus the compliance overhead, you're done, and you found out for free.

**3. The panel sees incumbents through the wrong end of the telescope, and the plan half-knows this.** Section 7 gets it right: panelists link their primary account, so a bank's own customers' internal flows are invisible. The implication the plan doesn't follow through: for an incumbent retail bank, what the panel observes is *outbound transfers from other people's primary accounts into that bank* — i.e. the bank as a secondary relationship. That population is small, unrepresentative, and its growth has no reliable relationship to reported deposits. So the nowcast test will fail for incumbents by construction, not because the mapping is wrong.

This compounds with a sample-size problem the plan never checks. The marquee feature is the NUTS3 location quotient. There are on the order of 1,300 NUTS3 regions in the EU. To get a stable geographic profile vector you need thousands of transacting panelists per ID. For a mid-cap Italian or Spanish bank in a panel with maybe low-hundreds-of-thousands of panelists in that country, you will have hundreds, not thousands. So the discriminating features require scale, the institutions with scale are the ones the panel sees worst, and the ones the panel sees well are private. That pincer is the project.

## Step-level breaks

**§3, label design.** 200–300 labels across six countries and five types is ~10 per cell. Sixty held out. You then propose to stratify holdout accuracy by country, type, size quartile *and* difficulty quintile. The confidence interval on rank-1 accuracy at n=60 is roughly ±12pp; on a size-quartile subset it's ±25pp. Your own kill criterion — "rank-1 below 65% for the top size quartile" — is not measurable at that sample size. Either raise the label count by an order of magnitude or abandon statistical framing entirely for case-by-case forensics.

**§3, label source (i) is the worst possible training set and the "difficulty score" doesn't fix it.** IDs whose strings leak leak for a systematic pipeline reason. A subjective 1–5 difficulty score does not let you reweight to the masked population. Two things would: inverse-propensity weighting, where you model P(labelled | observable ID features available for *all* IDs — size, country, type, morphology) and reweight the holdout; and a synthetic-mask control, where you hash the *named merchant* IDs and see whether the same pipeline recovers them. The second gives you thousands of unbiased labels on the machinery, at the cost that merchants aren't financials.

**§3, label source (ii) is used backwards.** A purchased named feed for one country is the only unbiased, complete, non-self-selected label set you will ever get. The plan reserves it strictly for training and forbids using it for validation in that country. That is exactly inverted. Train on the biased leak-set, *test* on the fully named country. Otherwise you have no honest read on precision anywhere.

**§4/§5, circularity between the matching model and the economic validation.** Stage two includes "Pearson correlation of monthly panel active customers against quarterly reported deposits" as a *pair-scoring feature*. Section 6 then presents nowcast performance as an independent economic test. It isn't independent of the model — only of the labels. If KPI correlation is an input to the match, discovering that matched IDs track KPIs is tautological. Fix: identify on geography, demographics, morphology, co-occurrence and events only; quarantine every reported-KPI relationship as pure validation. This costs match accuracy and it is not optional.

**§6, the nowcast test can pass under a wrong mapping.** European bank deposit growth is dominated by common factors — the rate cycle, national M2, term-deposit migration. Two banks in the same country have highly correlated YoY deposit growth. Mapping ID→Bank A when it is truly Bank B, same country, will still produce respectable pooled R². The permutation test is the right instinct but the design puts it in a vice: permute within loose strata and the test is uninformative; permute within country×type, as written, and the true mapping likely fails because the residual idiosyncratic signal is small. What actually discriminates is firm-specific timing: strip the country×type common factor from both series and test whether panel residuals predict reported residuals, and test whether the panel moves on the right dates for firm-specific events (a deposit promo, a rebrand, an outage, an acquisition close). Also: eight overlapping quarters with 2024–2025 evaluation is six to eight points per name. You cannot ship a per-name identification on a pooled fixed-effects R² — you ship names individually, so the evidence must be per-name.

**§4, event fingerprints — right family, wrong method, no null.** If a step change at the Bankia absorption is "nearly identifying on its own," then build the corporate-event calendar first and hand-search discontinuities. That gets you your first twenty IDs in three weeks with no labels, no logit and no calibration. As specified, PELT with BIC-tuned penalty on 24–48 monthly points will give you changepoints everywhere or nowhere, and ±45-day matching against a calendar of hundreds of events across 700 candidates will generate substantial coincidental matches. You must compute the null explicitly by shuffling the event calendar and reporting the spurious-match rate. Without that number, "matched changepoint count" is not evidence.

**§5, stages two and three are over-built.** Conditional logit assumes IIA, which twins violate directly — near-identical candidates split probability, both fall below 0.90, and your shippable set becomes precisely the names with no close peer. That's conservative and honest, but it means coverage is low and structurally non-random, which you should state up front rather than discover in week eleven. Isotonic calibration on a held-out fold of forty-odd observations will be an overfit step function; use Platt or beta, or drop calibrated-probability language and report empirical precision by margin decile. And cut stage three entirely. Hungarian assignment over 700 candidates buys nothing when you care about twenty names and when the plan already concedes injectivity fails for the co-op networks and multi-brand groups. It adds a failure mode where one bad match cascades.

**§5, the 700-way framing is the wrong construct.** You do not need to rank 700 candidates. You need to answer "is this ID Revolut / Trade Republic / Bank X?" for the names you would trade, with a strong "none of the above" prior and cheerfully low coverage. Reframing as a small set of binary hypothesis tests removes most of the statistical difficulty and all of stage three.

**§8, the timeline is off by a quarter.** Legal clearance *and* data delivery *and* the 700-row candidate table in weeks one to two is not real. European alt-data contracting plus DPIA plus environment onboarding is routinely two to three months. And branch-count-by-NUTS3 for 700 institutions is not a two-week job — OSM `operator` tagging is sparse and inconsistent outside DE/UK/FR.

**§3, unverified data assumptions that could end it on day three.** Is the hash per-counterparty or salted per-panelist? If per-panelist, wallet co-occurrence, ID-level aggregation and the whole approach die instantly. Is the hash stable across the 48-month window, or rotated? Does a clean "financial counterparty ID" field exist at all, or is it a mush of P2P transfers, ATM operators and processors — which is the usual reason this field gets stripped? What is the distinct-ID count and its size distribution, and panelists per country? Get a sample extract and answer these before anything else.

**Minor but fix them:** discarding hand-label disagreements rather than adjudicating deliberately manufactures the easy-label bias you elsewhere worry about — adjudicate, log it, and keep the disagreements as a hard-stratum test set. Kill criteria should be stated in tradeable terms ("≥N European-listed names at 95% precision *with* demonstrated per-name incremental skill over an app-download-plus-consensus baseline"), not "40 institutions," which forty private neobanks would satisfy while the project failed.

**One free-lunch check worth doing in week one:** for neobanks, app MAU and download data may already nowcast the KPI as well as the panel does. If so, the panel adds nothing over data you can buy far more cheaply, in the exact segment where identification is easiest. That's a one-week test on the handful of names with public customer-count disclosure, and it can kill the strongest-looking part of the project before you spend anything.

## The cheaper version

Five weeks, four sequential gates, stop at the first failure.

**Week 0 (two days).** Price a named financial-counterparty feed from the vendor and read the licence. Named feed available and affordable → buy it, project over. Explicitly prohibited → escalate to compliance and expect a no. Neither → continue.

**Week 1 (paper only, no data).** Enumerate the tradeable universe: European-listed financials with poor intra-quarter visibility, a panel-visible retail flow footprint, adequate country sample, and no indistinguishable twin. Attach an alpha estimate. Under ~15 names or under your cost hurdle → stop. Run the app-data-versus-panel test on any neobank with public disclosure while you're here.

**Weeks 2–3 (sample extract).** Hash stability and salting, ID count and size distribution, panelists per country, whether the financial-ID field is clean. Also compute, empirically, how many transacting panelists your ten largest target institutions actually have, and whether that supports a NUTS3 geography vector. Any of these fails → stop.

**Weeks 3–5 (forensics on the target list only).** Build the corporate-event calendar for your twenty names — this is the durable asset and the plan is right about that. Hand-match discontinuities: launches, market entries and exits, merger completions, rebrands, licence changes, rate-promo dates. Compute the shuffled-calendar null so you know your false-match rate. If you cannot confidently identify five of twenty names this way, the 200-feature model will not rescue you, because forensics is strictly easier than statistics here.

Only if all four gates pass do you commission the full build — and then with the leak-set bias correction, the KPI features quarantined out of matching, a fully-named country as the test set rather than the training set, per-name residual validation, and stage three deleted.

The honest summary is that this plan is a well-executed answer to a question that should have been dismantled commercially and legally before anyone designed a conditional logit. Find out whether there are fifteen tradeable names and whether you're allowed to do this at all. Both answers are available for the cost of a week.
