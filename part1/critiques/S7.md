# S7 — critique (claude-code subagent)

> Sell-side channel checks are incrementally positive on new logo wins. Should we expect a meaningful boost to full-year guidance at the upcoming print, and is this a long?

## Verdict

The plan is well above the median answer to this prompt. It splits the event question from the return question, it defines "meaningful" numerically instead of rhetorically, it leads with sizing arithmetic rather than data acquisition, and it knows that the firm's own guidance philosophy beats any cross-sectional panel. Those are the four things most analysts get wrong here, and this plan gets all four right. The compliance paragraph is not boilerplate and should stay.

The problem is not the reasoning. It is that the plan does not believe its own Step 1. Step 1 says, correctly, that this idea is probably dead on arithmetic — new logos are 10–30% of net new ARR, ratable recognition compresses the in-quarter effect to basis points, and the generous case likely fails the 0.5% bar. Then Steps 2–5 lay out three-to-four weeks of work (a 20-quarter point-in-time ledger, a 1,500–2,500 firm-quarter panel, 150–250 hand-coded notes, five alternative-data pulls) due Friday, on the assumption that Step 1 passed. It won't, most of the time. What you get on Friday is a half-built panel, a hand-coding job at 40% completion, and an analyst who has spent a week and therefore needs a trade.

Below are the specific breaks, then the version I would actually approve.

## What breaks

**1. Scope-to-decision mismatch. Step 4 is the wrong shape for this week.**
The panel is a research asset — it pays off across many future prints on many names. It is not an input to one event trade, and it cannot be built in the residual of one week. Worse, if Step 1 *passes*, you still don't need the panel to make the call: you need the firm's own ledger (Step 2) and the positioning map (Step 3). The panel is only load-bearing for the general claim "positive checks predict incremental raises," which is not the question the PM asked. Fix: cut Step 4 out of this week entirely, or shrink it to 15–25 hand-picked peer episodes used as a sanity anchor, not an estimate. If the desk wants the panel, fund it as a standing project with its own timeline and its own owner.

**2. The decision rule demands precision the design cannot produce.**
Gate #2 is "estimated P(incremental raise) exceeds the firm-specific base rate by at least 15 percentage points." Trace the numbers. The treated sample is ~150–250 episodes, then stratified by fiscal quarter (4 cells) *and* trailing flow-through regime (2–3 cells), leaving 15–40 observations per cell. A Jeffreys interval on a 40% rate with n=25 is roughly ±19pp. The firm-specific base rate on the other side of the comparison comes from 16–20 quarters of one company, of which perhaps 6–8 are positive-check quarters. You are differencing a noisy number against a noisier one and requiring the gap to clear 15pp. The plan flags small-N in Step 4 and then writes a gate that ignores it. Fix: state the minimum detectable effect before you start. If MDE > 15pp — it will be — then either drop the pretense that this gate is estimated and make it an explicit judgmental prior anchored on the ledger, or abandon the gate. Do not let a wide interval whose point estimate happens to clear 15pp be reported as a pass.

**3. The treatment variable is measured on an entitlement-selected archive, and the reliability check doesn't catch it.**
Hand-coding from "the fund's own broker note archive" means historical coverage is whatever the fund was paying for, on the names the fund cared about, in each year. Entitlements churn; notes get pulled; small-cap coverage is thin and lumpy. So "no positive check in the window" conflates *no check was published* with *we weren't entitled to the broker who published it*, and the conflation is correlated with exactly the thing you're studying (fund attention tracks narrative names). Cohen's kappa ≥ 0.7 tests whether two readers agree on notes you have. It says nothing about notes you don't. Fix: before coding anything, count notes per firm-quarter by year and restrict the panel to name-years above a minimum coverage threshold, so that absence means absence. Expect this to cut the universe hard, which is itself a finding worth knowing on Monday rather than Thursday.

**4. The scalable proxy is contaminated by construction, and the plan's tie-breaker misses the dangerous case.**
The proxy is "2+ upward pre-print revisions to the current-quarter estimate with no company disclosure in the window." That is close to a restatement of the outcome and it is *identical* to the crowding control in Step 3. Two consequences. First, pre-print upward revision momentum is a documented predictor of beats on its own; P(raise | proxy) will look strong and will be mostly the revision anomaly, not channel-check content. Second, the plan says "if they disagree, trust the hand-coded set" — but the failure mode is that they *agree*, directionally, for the wrong reason, and the plan has no test that fires in that case. Fix: the real test is whether hand-coded checks add anything *within* revision strata — i.e. among firm-quarters that already had 2+ upward revisions, does a positive check further raise P(incremental raise)? That is the only version that isolates check information. It also shrinks the effective sample again, which loops back to problem 2. Better to know that on Monday.

**5. The flow-through ratio blows up in exactly the quarters you have most of.**
Δ FY guide ÷ Q beat is undefined near a zero beat and flips sign on a miss. In a panel of software prints, a large minority have |beat| under ~0.2% of quarterly revenue. The plan uses this ratio as a stratifier, as a logit covariate, and as the "trailing four-quarter flow-through regime" that Step 2's whole narrative rests on. It will be garbage in the flattest quarters and will drive the stratification with outliers. Fix: use the *difference* in percent-of-FY-guide terms — which is what H1 already defines — everywhere, and never the ratio. Keep the ratio as expositional color for the write-up only.

**6. H1 is a one-dimensional outcome for a multi-dimensional event.**
You can be exactly right — FY revenue midpoint raised more than the beat, clears 0.5%, survives FX — and lose money. Three routes: cRPO or billings decelerates and that is what the tape trades in subscription software; the implied Q4 gets cut to fund the raise; or the FY operating margin guide comes down. That last one is not a tail case — it is the *mechanism* the checks describe. New-logo-heavy growth is margin-dilutive (S&M load, implementation drag, land-then-expand economics), so a genuine new-logo surge argues for a better revenue guide and a worse EPS guide simultaneously. The plan's Step 5 half-notices this ("logo count at smaller land sizes is consistent with weaker, not stronger") but H1 and the decision rule never absorb it. Fix: pre-register the outcome as a short vector — FY revenue, implied next quarter, cRPO/billings, FY operating margin — with a predicted sign on each, and require the trade to survive the joint case, not just the revenue leg.

**7. Step 5's "three of five proxies agree" has no power.**
With five noisy roughly-binary proxies, the chance that at least three point the same direction under the null is around a coin flip. As a gate it will essentially always pass, and passing it will feel like corroboration. The individual proxies are also weaker than presented: Wayback logo-page snapshots record marketing decisions, not signed customers; G2 and Peer Insights review counts are campaign-driven and vendors run review drives around renewals and product launches; CS/implementation headcount is usually a fiscal-year planning decision, not a response to last month's bookings. Fix: drop the vote count. Pick the two proxies you can *backtest against this name's own last 12 quarters* of reported revenue or disclosed customer counts, establish the lead-lag and the noise level empirically, and use only those. That is cheap, checkable, and tells you what a given proxy reading is actually worth.

**8. The positioning-trade escape hatch is unmodelled and it is the branch the analyst will take.**
The decision rule permits, when "the arithmetic is thin but positioning is washed out," a third-size short-horizon positioning trade closed within two days. Nothing in Steps 1–5 estimates the return to that trade — not the base rate, not the hit rate, not the payoff versus the implied move. It is a free-floating discretionary override bolted onto an otherwise pre-registered process, and given that Step 1 will usually kill the arithmetic, it is the branch that gets used. Fix: delete it, or promote it to a third hypothesis with its own falsifier and its own pre-computed base rate (post-print reaction conditional on washed-out setups) done *before* Friday. As written it converts a disciplined plan into "we found a reason to be involved."

**9. The 0.5% threshold is asserted, not calibrated to the price.**
"Meaningful" is defined against the FY guide, but the question is whether it moves the stock. Fix, and it is a 30-minute fix: from Step 2's ledger, regress or just scatter the [0,+1] move against the incremental-raise measure across the last 16 prints for this name. That gives you the incremental raise associated with a one-standard-deviation reaction. If it's 1.2%, then 0.5% is not the bar and H1 is testing the wrong quantity.

**10. The regime robustness check has nowhere to stand.**
Five to seven years of history minus 2020–2021 leaves roughly 2022–2026, which is one regime: rate shock, software derating, seat-growth stall, then AI budget reallocation. Running the panel with and without COVID gives you two samples, each internally homogeneous and mutually incomparable. This is not fixable by a robustness table. It is fixable only by lowering the confidence attached to the panel result — which is another argument for problem 1.

**11. Two smaller items.** The "buy-side bar sits 100–200bps above consensus" in Step 3 is a stylized fact used as if it were a measurement; either pull the desk's own history of broker bogeys versus outcomes for this name or leave it qualitative. And "FX contribution as disclosed" will be a sparse column — most issuers give constant-currency growth rates, not a dollar FX bridge on guidance — so the FX-survival requirement in H1 needs a stated fallback (reconstruct from the CC growth guide and the revenue mix) or it becomes a silent exclusion filter.

**12. On pre-registration.** It is the right instinct and I want it kept, but the plan oversells it. Pre-registering one forecast on one print yields one bit of evidence. It makes the exercise *honest*, not *cumulative*. Cumulative requires the desk doing this on every event for a year. Worth saying plainly so nobody mistakes a timestamped file for a track record.

## Is the idea worth a full project?

No. Not as stated, and the plan's own Step 1 is the reason. A signal about the 10–30% of net new ARR that comes from new logos, recognized ratably, mid-quarter, is a basis-point-scale claim on a full-year number. Layer on the fact that these are firm-approved third-party notes — every entitled fund has them — and the informational edge is close to zero by construction. The plan is correct that the only version with real edge is proprietary checks, and correct not to go there.

**The cheaper version, half a day:**

1. **The arithmetic** (90 minutes, 10-K + last four 8-Ks + a legal pad). New-logo ACV, new-logo share of net new ARR, recognition lag. Generous case as a percent of the FY guide. This is Step 1 unchanged — it is the best thing in the plan.
2. **The ledger, this name only, 12 quarters** (3 hours from EDGAR). Prior guide, consensus, print, new guide, incremental raise in percent-of-FY-guide terms, fiscal-quarter flag, CFO tenure. Add the [0,+1] move column so you get the price calibration in item 9 for free. The question it answers: has this CFO *ever* raised by more than the beat, and in which fiscal quarter?
3. **What's priced** (2 hours). 30-day FY revision trajectory versus the company guide, 20-day return versus a 6–10 name peer basket, ATM straddle implied move versus trailing-12 realized [0,+1] absolute moves.

Three numbers: does the signal size matter, does this management team ever flow through above 1x, and is it already in the price. If the answer to any one is no, write the page and move on. That is the whole decision, and it does not require a panel, a Firth logit, or a hand-coding project.

Two structural additions the cheap version should carry over from the full plan: the multi-outcome pre-registration from problem 6 (revenue, next-Q implied, cRPO, margin — with signs), and the compliance log.

And answer the horizon question first, on Monday, as the plan says on line 9 and then doesn't do. If the intent is a multi-quarter long, none of this work speaks to it — channel checks have a half-life of days — and the honest response is that the long case has to be made on NRR trajectory, competitive displacement, unit economics, and valuation, with the print as an entry-timing question at most. The plan raises this and then quietly answers the event question instead because that's the one it has tools for. Don't let that stand.

## What I'd approve

Half a day on items 1–3 above, with a Wednesday check-in rather than a Friday deliverable. Panel work moved to a separate proposal if the desk wants it, judged on its own merits as infrastructure. Positioning-trade override deleted from the decision rule unless someone brings me a base rate for it first.
